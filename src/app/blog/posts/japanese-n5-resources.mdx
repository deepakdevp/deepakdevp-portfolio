# The N5 Toolkit That Actually Works

After weeks of trying different resources, I've settled on a stack that actually works for my learning style. Here's my honest review of what I use daily.

## Daily Drivers

### 1. Anki (Free) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The GOAT of spaced repetition. I use it for:
- **Hiragana/Katakana** — Core 2k/6k deck (first 1000 cards)
- **Vocabulary** — JLPT Tango N5 deck
- **Kanji** — Recognition only, not writing

**My settings:**
- New cards/day: 10
- Review limit: 100
- Steps: 15m, 1d, 3d

**Verdict:** Non-negotiable. This is how vocabulary sticks.

### 2. Bunpro (¥1,500/month) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

SRS for grammar. Each grammar point has:
- Multiple example sentences
- Cloze deletion practice
- Links to external explanations

**What I like:**
- Forces you to produce Japanese, not just recognize it
- Ghost reviews for mistakes
- JLPT-organized paths

**What I don't:**
- Can feel repetitive
- Some explanations are thin

**Verdict:** Worth it if you're serious about grammar. Pairs perfectly with Anki for vocab.

### 3. Minna no Nihongo (¥2,500) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The textbook my wife used. It's old-school but comprehensive.

**Why it works:**
- Structured progression (no jumping around)
- Audio CDs for listening practice
- Workbook for writing practice
- Used by most Japanese language schools

**My approach:**
- 1 chapter per week
- All exercises in the workbook
- Claude Code generates extra practice sentences

**Verdict:** The foundation. Everything else supplements this.

## Supplementary Resources

### 4. JapanesePod101 (Free tier) ⭐⭐⭐

Podcast-style lessons. Good for:
- Listening practice during commute
- Cultural context
- Casual learning days

**The catch:** Free tier is limited. Premium is expensive ($25/month). I stick to free.

### 5. Tae Kim's Grammar Guide (Free) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Free online grammar reference. When Bunpro's explanation confuses me, Tae Kim usually clarifies.

**Best for:**
- Looking up specific grammar points
- Understanding the "why" behind rules
- Casual reading

### 6. NHK World Easy Japanese (Free) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Real news articles written for learners. Good for:
- Reading practice with furigana
- Current events in simple Japanese
- Feeling like you're using "real" Japanese

## What I Skip

### WaniKani
Great for kanji, but:
- Expensive ($9/month)
- Locked progression (can't skip what you know)
- Kanji focus is overkill for N5 (only ~100 kanji needed)

I use Anki + RTK for kanji instead.

### Duolingo
Fun but inefficient. The gamification is addictive, not effective. After 2 weeks I realized I wasn't actually learning.

### Random YouTube Channels
Too scattered. I watch occasionally for motivation but don't count it as study time.

## My Study Schedule

\`\`\`
Morning (30 min):
- Anki reviews (vocab + kanji)
- Bunpro reviews (grammar)

Evening (45 min):
- Minna no Nihongo (new content)
- Workbook exercises
- Claude Code conversation practice

Weekend (2 hours):
- Catch up on reviews
- NHK Easy reading
- Practice with my wife
\`\`\`

## The Secret Weapon: Claude Code

I didn't expect this, but Claude Code has become my most valuable study partner:

1. **Custom practice sentences** — "Give me 10 sentences using て-form with workplace vocabulary"
2. **Instant corrections** — Catches mistakes I'd miss
3. **Conversation simulation** — Practices scenarios before real conversations
4. **Progress tracking** — Saves everything to Notion automatically

It's like having a tutor available 24/7.

## What Actually Matters

After trying many resources, here's what I've learned:

1. **Consistency > Tools** — 30 minutes daily beats 4 hours on weekends
2. **Production > Recognition** — Speaking and writing stick better than just reading
3. **Boring works** — Flashcards aren't exciting, but they work
4. **Human practice is irreplaceable** — My wife catches things no app would

If I had to start over with only 3 resources: **Anki + Minna no Nihongo + a patient human**.
