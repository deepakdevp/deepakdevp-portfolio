# はじめまして！(Nice to meet you!)

Okay, I'll be honest — I've been *meaning* to learn Japanese for about two years. I've had Duolingo streaks that lasted three days. I've watched anime thinking it would magically absorb into my brain. It didn't.

But this time feels different, because I have a real reason: I'm planning to move to Japan.

So I made a commitment — **JLPT N5 by the end of this year**. That's the beginner level, but it's still 800+ vocabulary words, grammar foundations, and reading basic kanji. Real work.

## Week 1: What I covered

**Hiragana** — I decided to start with the full hiragana chart rather than jumping into phrases. There are 46 characters and I thought this would take weeks. Turns out, with spaced repetition and about 30 minutes a day, I had all of them recognisable by day 5. Not perfect, but readable.

Some that tripped me up:
- **ぬ** vs **め** — they look almost identical to me
- **は** is pronounced "ha" normally but "wa" when used as a topic particle
- **へ** is "he" but as a direction particle it's "e"

Japanese logic is... interesting.

**First phrases I learned:**
- こんにちは — Konnichiwa (Hello / Good afternoon)
- ありがとう — Arigatou (Thank you)
- すみません — Sumimasen (Excuse me / Sorry)
- わかりません — Wakarimasen (I don't understand)

That last one might be the most useful for the next few months.

## What's actually working

1. **Anki** — Spaced repetition is real. I add 10–15 new cards per day and review everything that comes up. It's boring but effective.

2. **Writing by hand** — Sounds old school but physically writing hiragana helps. Muscle memory is a thing.

3. **Using OpenClaw for practice** — I've been asking my AI assistant to quiz me in Discord. It'll throw a hiragana character at me and I have to type the romaji. Simple but it keeps me honest.

## What's hard

Honestly? **Everything sounds fast.** Native Japanese speech is incredibly fast to my ears right now. I watch YouTube videos on 0.75x speed and still miss words. I'm told this gets better. I choose to believe that.

Also — I need to learn katakana next, and then kanji starts. There are 2,136 "daily use" kanji characters in Japanese. I'm trying not to think about that.

## My plan going forward

- Hiragana: ✅ done
- Katakana: this week
- Grammar N5 basics: starting next week (は、が、を、に particles)
- Vocabulary: 10 new words/day via Anki
- Listening: 15 minutes of easy Japanese YouTube daily

I'll keep writing these updates weekly. If you're also learning Japanese, drop me a message — it's way more fun when you're not doing it alone.

頑張ります！(I'll do my best!)
