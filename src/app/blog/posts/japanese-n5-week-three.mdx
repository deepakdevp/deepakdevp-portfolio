# 最初の会話！(First Conversation!)

Three weeks in. Today I had my first real conversation in Japanese — not scripted, not rehearsed, just... talking. It was terrible. It was amazing.

## The Setup

My wife's colleague visited our place. She's Japanese, speaks limited English. Perfect test subject.

**My goal:** Survive 5 minutes of small talk without switching to English.

## How It Actually Went

### The Good Parts

**Self-introduction worked:**
\`\`\`
私はディーパックです。
エンジニアです。
インドから来ました。
\`\`\`

She understood! I could see the recognition in her eyes. Three weeks of drilling paid off.

**Simple questions worked:**
\`\`\`
日本語は難しいですか？
(Nihongo wa muzukashii desu ka?)
Is Japanese difficult?

はい、でも面白いです。
(Hai, demo omoshiroi desu.)
Yes, but it's interesting.
\`\`\`

She laughed and said my pronunciation was "kawaii" (cute). I'll take it.

### The Rough Parts

**Past tense broke me:**

She asked when I started learning. I knew the answer but couldn't conjugate:

\`\`\`
What I wanted to say:
三週間前に始めました。
(Sanshūkan mae ni hajimemashita.)
I started three weeks ago.

What I actually said:
三週間... 前... 始める... です？
(Confused word salad)
\`\`\`

My wife jumped in to save me. But honestly? The fact that I *tried* felt like a win.

**Vocabulary gaps are brutal:**

She mentioned something about her weekend. I caught maybe 40% of the words. The rest was just sounds.

I nodded a lot. Smiled. Said "そうですか" (is that so?) approximately 47 times.

## What I Learned

### 1. Production is 10x Harder Than Recognition

Reading Japanese? I'm okay. Hearing Japanese? I understand the gist. *Speaking* Japanese in real-time? My brain short-circuits.

The gap between passive and active knowledge is massive.

### 2. Grammar Matters More Than Vocabulary

I know maybe 200 words. That's enough for basic conversation *if* you can arrange them correctly. Grammar is the bottleneck.

Priorities shifting: More grammar drills, less vocabulary cramming.

### 3. Confidence is Half the Battle

My wife later told me I was speaking more correctly than I thought. My brain was too panicked to register it.

Next time: breathe, slow down, trust the practice.

## Grammar Points That Saved Me

### て-form for Connecting

\`\`\`
日本語を勉強して、楽しいです。
(Nihongo wo benkyou shite, tanoshii desu.)
I study Japanese and it's fun.
\`\`\`

### から for Giving Reasons

\`\`\`
日本が好きですから。
(Nihon ga suki desu kara.)
Because I like Japan.
\`\`\`

### けど for Contrast

\`\`\`
難しいですけど、頑張ります。
(Muzukashii desu kedo, ganbarimasu.)
It's difficult, but I'll do my best.
\`\`\`

These three patterns carried 80% of the conversation.
