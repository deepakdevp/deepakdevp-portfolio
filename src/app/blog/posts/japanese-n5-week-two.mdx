# 週2完了！(Week 2 complete!)

Last week hiragana. This week — katakana. Honestly? Katakana was harder for me than hiragana, and I think I know why.

## Why katakana felt different

Hiragana characters are round and flowing. Katakana is angular and sharp. My brain kept confusing:
- **ソ** (so) vs **ン** (n) — tilted differently but look like twins
- **シ** (shi) vs **ツ** (tsu) — same problem, different angle
- **ウ** (u) vs **フ** (fu) — one curve changes everything

But here's the thing about katakana: once you crack it, you can immediately read a bunch of words. Because katakana is used for foreign loanwords. So:
- コーヒー = ko-hi- = Coffee ☕
- テレビ = te-re-bi = Television 📺
- アイスクリーム = a-i-su-ku-ri-mu = Ice cream 🍦

This made practice way more satisfying. Every word I decoded felt like a small win.

## First particles: は、が、を、に

This is where grammar started. The big four N5 particles:

| Particle | Romaji | Role |
|----------|--------|------|
| は | wa | Topic marker |
| が | ga | Subject marker |
| を | o | Object marker |
| に | ni | Direction/time |

The は vs が distinction is already breaking my brain. Japanese natives describe the difference as "feel" which is... unhelpful. But I'll get there.

## First sentence I wrote

> 私はエンジニアです。
> *Watashi wa enjinia desu.*
> "I am an engineer."

Simple. But it felt like a milestone. I constructed it myself without looking up the structure.
