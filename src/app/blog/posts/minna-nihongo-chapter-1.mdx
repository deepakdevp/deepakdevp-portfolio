## です (desu): The Foundation

Everything in Chapter 1 builds on **です**, which means "is/am/are" for nouns.

**Pattern:** [noun] + です

Examples:
- 私はディーパクです。(Watashi wa Dīpaku desu) - I am Deepak
- これは本です。(Kore wa hon desu) - This is a book
- あの人は田中さんです。(Ano hito wa Tanaka-san desu) - That person is Tanaka-san

My wife corrected my pronunciation immediately: "It's 'dess', not 'des-oo'. The う is almost silent."

This was huge. I'd been saying "dess-oo" for weeks. Native speakers drop that final う sound.

## Particles: The Glue of Japanese

Chapter 1 introduces three essential particles:

### は (wa) - Topic Marker

Despite being written は (ha), it's pronounced "wa" when used as a particle.

- 私**は**学生です。(Watashi **wa** gakusei desu) - I am a student

The topic marker says "speaking of [X]". So this sentence literally means: "Speaking of me, (I) am a student."

### の (no) - Possessive

Links two nouns to show possession or relationship.

- 私**の**本 (watashi **no** hon) - my book
- 会社**の**人 (kaisha **no** hito) - company person (colleague)

### か (ka) - Question Marker

Turns any statement into a question. Just add か to the end.

- 学生です。(Gakusei desu) - (I) am a student
- 学生です**か**。(Gakusei desu **ka**) - Are (you) a student?

No need to change word order like in English. Mind = blown.

## Self-Introduction: The Real Goal

Here's the Chapter 1 self-introduction formula:

```
はじめまして。(Hajimemashite)
私は [name] です。
[nationality/occupation] です。
どうぞよろしくお願いします。(Dōzo yoroshiku onegaishimasu)
```

My introduction:
```
はじめまして。
私はディーパクです。
インド人です。
エンジニアです。
どうぞよろしくお願いします。
```

Translation:
> Nice to meet you.
> I am Deepak.
> I am Indian.
> I am an engineer.
> Please treat me well. (idiomatic: "Nice to meet you")

My wife made me practice this 20 times until the rhythm felt natural. "Japanese is about rhythm and flow, not just words," she said.

## Vocabulary: Workplace Edition

Chapter 1 has standard vocab (student, teacher, company employee), but I wanted **workplace-specific practice**. Claude Code helped me create custom examples:

**Occupations:**
- エンジニア (enjinia) - engineer
- プログラマー (puroguramā) - programmer
- デザイナー (dezainā) - designer
- マネージャー (manējā) - manager

**Companies:**
- 会社 (kaisha) - company
- 会社員 (kaishain) - company employee
- IT会社 (IT kaisha) - IT company

**Sample conversations Claude Code generated:**

```
A: はじめまして。山田です。
   エンジニアです。

B: はじめまして。ディーパクです。
   私もエンジニアです。
   よろしくお願いします。

A: こちらこそ、よろしくお願いします。
```

Translation:
> A: Nice to meet you. I'm Yamada. I'm an engineer.
> B: Nice to meet you. I'm Deepak. I'm also an engineer. Nice to meet you.
> A: Likewise, nice to meet you.

That **も (mo)** in there means "also/too". Instead of は, you use も:
- 私**は**エンジニアです - I am an engineer
- 私**も**エンジニアです - I am **also** an engineer

## Negative Form: じゃありません

To say "is not," use **じゃありません (ja arimasen)**.

- 学生です (gakusei desu) - (I) am a student
- 学生じゃありません (gakusei ja arimasen) - (I) am not a student

My wife added: "You can also say じゃないです (ja nai desu), which is slightly more casual but still polite. Both are fine for N5."

**Practice sentences:**
- 私は学生じゃありません。会社員です。
  (I am not a student. I am a company employee.)

- これは本じゃありません。ノートです。
  (This is not a book. It is a notebook.)

## Cultural Notes from My Wife

My wife added context that the textbook didn't cover:

**1. Name order:**
- Japanese: Family name first (田中太郎 - Tanaka Tarō)
- Western: Given name first (Deepak Panwar)
- When speaking Japanese, use Japanese order for Japanese names

**2. -san (さん):**
- Use with everyone except yourself
- Never say 私はディーパクさんです (I am Deepak-san) ← Wrong!
- Always use with others: 田中さん (Tanaka-san)

**3. よろしくお願いします:**
- Literally "please treat me favorably"
- Used at the end of introductions
- Also used when asking favors, starting projects, etc.
- Super versatile phrase—learn it well

**4. Bowing:**
- In Japan, you bow when saying はじめまして
- Depth of bow = level of respect
- For peers: slight nod
- For superiors: deeper bow (about 30°)

These cultural notes make the language feel *real*, not just grammar rules.

## My First Real Japanese Conversation

At the end of the week, my wife arranged a surprise: a video call with her Japanese friend who wanted to practice English.

We started in Japanese:

```
Friend: はじめまして。由美です。日本人です。よろしくお願いします。

Me: はじめまして。ディーパクです。インド人です。エンジニアです。どうぞよろしくお願いします。

Friend: エンジニアですか。すごいですね！

Me: (panic—I didn't understand すごい)
```

My wife whispered: "She said 'that's amazing!'"

```
Me: ありがとうございます！(Thank you very much!)
```

We switched to English after that, but **I successfully introduced myself in Japanese to a native speaker.** Chapter 1 complete!
