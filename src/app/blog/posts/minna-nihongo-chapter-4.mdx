# Minna no Nihongo Chapter 4: Time, Numbers, and Schedules

I thought I knew Japanese numbers. I'd learned 1-10 in hiragana weeks ago. Easy, right?

**Wrong.**

Chapter 4 revealed that Japanese has a completely different counting system depending on **what you're counting**. Want to count people? Different counter. Books? Different counter. Long thin objects? You guessed it—different counter.

And don't even get me started on telling time. "4 o'clock" is **よじ (yo-ji)**, but "4 minutes" is **よんぷん (yon-pun)**. Why? Because Japanese.

This chapter took me a week and a half. But by the end, I could:
- Tell time accurately
- Describe my work schedule
- Count (most) things correctly
- Ask "when?" and understand the answer

That's worth the struggle.

## Part 1: Numbers Review

First, the basics. Japanese has two number systems:

### Native Japanese (1-10)
- ひとつ (hitotsu) - one
- ふたつ (futatsu) - two
- みっつ (mittsu) - three
- よっつ (yottsu) - four
- いつつ (itsutsu) - five
- むっつ (muttsu) - six
- ななつ (nanatsu) - seven
- やっつ (yattsu) - eight
- ここのつ (kokonotsu) - nine
- とお (tō) - ten

### Sino-Japanese (general counting)
- いち (ichi) - 1
- に (ni) - 2
- さん (san) - 3
- し/よん (shi/yon) - 4
- ご (go) - 5
- ろく (roku) - 6
- しち/なな (shichi/nana) - 7
- はち (hachi) - 8
- きゅう/く (kyū/ku) - 9
- じゅう (jū) - 10

Chapter 4 uses **Sino-Japanese** for time, dates, and most counting.

## Part 2: Telling Time

This is where Chapter 4 gets practical immediately.

### Hours: 〜時 (〜ji)

Pattern: [number] + 時 (ji)

```
1時 - いちじ (ichi-ji) - 1 o'clock
2時 - にじ (ni-ji) - 2 o'clock
3時 - さんじ (san-ji) - 3 o'clock
4時 - よじ (yo-ji) - 4 o'clock ← Special!
5時 - ごじ (go-ji) - 5 o'clock
6時 - ろくじ (roku-ji) - 6 o'clock
7時 - しちじ (shichi-ji) - 7 o'clock
8時 - はちじ (hachi-ji) - 8 o'clock
9時 - くじ (ku-ji) - 9 o'clock ← Special!
10時 - じゅうじ (jū-ji) - 10 o'clock
11時 - じゅういちじ (jūichi-ji) - 11 o'clock
12時 - じゅうにじ (jūni-ji) - 12 o'clock
```

**Key irregularities:**
- 4 o'clock = **よじ** (not しじ or よんじ)
- 9 o'clock = **くじ** (not きゅうじ)

My wife explained: "よん and きゅう sound better with じ. It's about phonetics, not logic. Just memorize it."

### Minutes: 〜分 (〜pun/fun)

This is where it gets complicated. The pronunciation changes based on the number:

```
1分 - いっぷん (ippun)
2分 - にふん (ni-fun)
3分 - さんぷん (san-pun)
4分 - よんぷん (yon-pun)
5分 - ごふん (go-fun)
6分 - ろっぷん (roppun)
7分 - ななふん (nana-fun)
8分 - はっぷん (happun)
9分 - きゅうふん (kyū-fun)
10分 - じゅっぷん (juppun)
```

**Pattern:**
- 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10 → ぷん (pun)
- 2, 5, 7, 9 → ふん (fun)

Claude Code made me drill this 50 times. It's pure memorization.

### Full Time Examples

```
3時15分 - さんじじゅうごふん (san-ji jūgo-fun) - 3:15
7時30分 - しちじさんじゅっぷん (shichi-ji sanjuppun) - 7:30
9時45分 - くじよんじゅうごふん (ku-ji yonjūgo-fun) - 9:45
12時00分 - じゅうにじ (jūni-ji) - 12:00
```

**半 (han)** = half (30 minutes):
```
3時半 - さんじはん (san-ji han) - 3:30
```

## Part 3: Time Particles

### に (ni) - Specific Time

Use に after specific times:

```
7時に起きます。
(Shichi-ji ni okimasu)
I wake up at 7 o'clock.

12時半に昼ご飯を食べます。
(Jūni-ji han ni hiru-gohan o tabemasu)
I eat lunch at 12:30.
```

**But not with relative time words:**

```
❌ 今日に行きます (wrong!)
✅ 今日行きます (kyō ikimasu) - I go today

❌ 毎日に勉強します (wrong!)
✅ 毎日勉強します (mainichi benkyō shimasu) - I study every day
```

Words that **don't** take に:
- 今日 (kyō) - today
- 明日 (ashita) - tomorrow
- 昨日 (kinō) - yesterday
- 毎日 (mainichi) - every day
- 今週 (konshū) - this week

### から/まで (kara/made) - From/Until

```
9時から5時まで働きます。
(Ku-ji kara go-ji made hatarakimasu)
I work from 9 to 5.

月曜日から金曜日まで会社へ行きます。
(Getsuyōbi kara kin'yōbi made kaisha e ikimasu)
I go to the company from Monday to Friday.
```

## Part 4: Days of the Week

Essential for describing schedules:

- 月曜日 (getsuyōbi) - Monday
- 火曜日 (kayōbi) - Tuesday
- 水曜日 (suiyōbi) - Wednesday
- 木曜日 (mokuyōbi) - Thursday
- 金曜日 (kin'yōbi) - Friday
- 土曜日 (doyōbi) - Saturday
- 日曜日 (nichiyōbi) - Sunday

**Mnemonic from my wife:**
- 月 (moon/Monday)
- 火 (fire/Tuesday)
- 水 (water/Wednesday)
- 木 (tree/Thursday)
- 金 (gold/Friday)
- 土 (earth/Saturday)
- 日 (sun/Sunday)

Each character represents an element. Kind of poetic.

## Part 5: Counter Words

Here's where Japanese gets wild. You can't just say "three books"—you need to use the right **counter** for books.

### Common Counters

**〜つ (tsu)** - General counter (1-10):
```
りんごをひとつください。
(Ringo o hitotsu kudasai)
One apple, please.

みっつあります。
(Mittsu arimasu)
There are three.
```

**〜人 (nin/ri)** - People:
```
1人 - ひとり (hitori) ← Irregular!
2人 - ふたり (futari) ← Irregular!
3人 - さんにん (san-nin)
4人 - よにん (yo-nin)
5人 - ごにん (go-nin)
```

**〜冊 (satsu)** - Books/magazines:
```
本を2冊買いました。
(Hon o ni-satsu kaimashita)
I bought two books.
```

**〜枚 (mai)** - Flat objects (paper, shirts, plates):
```
紙を3枚ください。
(Kami o san-mai kudasai)
Three sheets of paper, please.
```

**〜本 (hon/pon/bon)** - Long cylindrical objects (pens, bottles, umbrellas):
```
ペンが4本あります。
(Pen ga yon-hon arimasu)
There are four pens.
```

### The 本 Counter: Phonetic Changes

This counter changes pronunciation:

- 1本 - いっぽん (ippon)
- 2本 - にほん (ni-hon)
- 3本 - さんぼん (san-bon)
- 4本 - よんほん (yon-hon)
- 5本 - ごほん (go-hon)
- 6本 - ろっぽん (roppon)
- 7本 - ななほん (nana-hon)
- 8本 - はっぽん (happon)
- 9本 - きゅうほん (kyū-hon)
- 10本 - じゅっぽん (juppon)

Claude Code made me practice this until I could say it without thinking. It's muscle memory at this point.

```
(Getsuyōbi kara kin'yōbi made hatarakimasu)
I work from Monday to Friday.

朝7時に家を出ます。
(Asa shichi-ji ni uchi o demasu)
I leave home at 7 AM.

(Hachi-ji kara ku-ji made tsūkin shimasu)
I commute from 8 to 9.

9時から12時まで会社で働きます。
(Ku-ji kara jūni-ji made kaisha de hatarakimasu)
I work at the company from 9 to 12.

12時から1時まで昼ご飯を食べます。
(Jūni-ji kara ichi-ji made hiru-gohan o tabemasu)
I eat lunch from 12 to 1.

1時から6時まで働きます。
(Ichi-ji kara roku-ji made hatarakimasu)
I work from 1 to 6.

7時ごろ家へ帰ります。
(Shichi-ji goro uchi e kaerimasu)
I return home around 7.

(Doyōbi to nichiyōbi wa yasumi desu)
Saturday and Sunday are my days off.
```

**This is my actual schedule in Japanese!**

## Question Words: いつ/何時/何曜日

### いつ (itsu) - When?

```
A: いつ東京へ行きますか。
   (Itsu Tōkyō e ikimasu ka)
   When do you go to Tokyo?

B: 来週行きます。
   (Raishū ikimasu)
   I go next week.
```

### 何時 (nan-ji) - What time?

```
A: 何時に起きますか。
   (Nan-ji ni okimasu ka)
   What time do you wake up?

B: 6時に起きます。
   (Roku-ji ni okimasu)
   I wake up at 6.
```

### 何曜日 (nan-yōbi) - What day of the week?

```
A: 何曜日に休みますか。
   (Nan-yōbi ni yasumimasu ka)
   What day do you have off?

B: 土曜日と日曜日に休みます。
   (Doyōbi to nichiyōbi ni yasumimasu)
   I have off on Saturday and Sunday.
```

## Real-World Application

I used Chapter 4 skills to schedule a meeting (in Japanese!) with my wife's friend:

```
Me: 今週、会いませんか。
    (Konshū, aimasen ka)
    Shall we meet this week?

Friend: いいですよ。いつがいいですか。
        (Ii desu yo. Itsu ga ii desu ka)
        Sure! When is good?

Me: 土曜日はどうですか。
    (Doyōbi wa dō desu ka)
    How about Saturday?

Friend: いいですね。何時がいいですか。
        (Ii desu ne. Nan-ji ga ii desu ka)
        Good! What time is good?

Me: 3時はどうですか。
    (San-ji wa dō desu ka)
    How about 3 o'clock?

Friend: 3時は大丈夫です。じゃあ、土曜日の3時に会いましょう。
        (San-ji wa daijōbu desu. Jā, doyōbi no san-ji ni aimashō)
        3 is fine. Then, let's meet at 3 on Saturday.

Me: はい、分かりました！
    (Hai, wakarimashita)
    Yes, understood!
```

**I scheduled a meeting in Japanese!** This felt like a massive win.

## Common Mistakes I Made

### Mistake 1: Using に with relative time

❌ 今日に行きます
✅ 今日行きます

**Fix:** No に for 今日、明日、昨日、毎日, etc.

### Mistake 2: Wrong hour pronunciation

❌ よんじ (for 4 o'clock)
✅ よじ

❌ きゅうじ (for 9 o'clock)
✅ くじ

**Fix:** Just memorize the exceptions.

### Mistake 3: Forgetting counter changes

❌ さんほん (for 3 pens)
✅ さんぼん (hon → bon after ん sound)

**Fix:** Practice counters until phonetic changes are automatic.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Time in Japanese requires memorization** - Irregularities in hours and minutes
2. **Particles matter for time** - に for specific times, but not relative ones
3. **Counter words are language-specific** - No universal "1, 2, 3" for all objects
4. **Phonetic changes are predictable** - hon → bon, hon → pon follow patterns
5. **Real-world practice cements learning** - Scheduling meetings > 100 textbook drills
