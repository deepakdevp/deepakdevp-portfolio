# みんなの日本語 Chapter 5

This chapter clicked for me in a big way. Before this, I could describe *things* and *people*, but I couldn't say **where they are** or **where they're going**. Chapter 5 fixes that with movement and existence verbs.

## Movement Verbs: 行く、来る、帰る

These three verbs are how you talk about going places:

### 行く (いく, iku) — "to go"

\`\`\`
会社に行きます。
(Kaisha ni ikimasu.)
I go to the office.

東京に行きました。
(Tokyo ni ikimashita.)
I went to Tokyo.
\`\`\`

### 来る (くる, kuru) — "to come"

\`\`\`
友達が来ます。
(Tomodachi ga kimasu.)
My friend is coming.

日本に来ました。
(Nihon ni kimashita.)
I came to Japan.
\`\`\`

### 帰る (かえる, kaeru) — "to return/go back"

\`\`\`
家に帰ります。
(Ie ni kaerimasu.)
I return home.

6時に帰りました。
(Rokuji ni kaerimashita.)
I went back at 6 o'clock.
\`\`\`

### Key Pattern: [Place] に [Verb]

The particle **に (ni)** marks the **destination**.

\`\`\`
[Where] に 行く/来る/帰る
\`\`\`

**Examples:**
\`\`\`
スーパーに行きます。  (I go to the supermarket.)
オフィスに来ます。    (I come to the office.)
インドに帰ります。    (I return to India.)
\`\`\`

## Existence Verbs: います vs あります

Japanese has two "to be" verbs for existence, depending on whether the thing is **animate** or **inanimate**.

### います (imasu) — For Living Things

People, animals, insects — anything that moves on its own.

\`\`\`
猫がいます。
(Neko ga imasu.)
There is a cat. / A cat exists.

田中さんはオフィスにいます。
(Tanaka-san wa ofisu ni imasu.)
Tanaka-san is in the office.
\`\`\`

### あります (arimasu) — For Non-Living Things

Objects, buildings, events — anything that doesn't move on its own.

\`\`\`
本があります。
(Hon ga arimasu.)
There is a book. / A book exists.

会議が3時にあります。
(Kaigi ga sanji ni arimasu.)
There is a meeting at 3 o'clock.
\`\`\`

### Key Pattern: [Thing] が [Place] に います/あります

\`\`\`
[What] が [Where] に います/あります
\`\`\`

**Examples:**
\`\`\`
机の上にパソコンがあります。
(Tsukue no ue ni pasokon ga arimasu.)
There is a computer on the desk.

部屋に人がいます。
(Heya ni hito ga imasu.)
There is a person in the room.
\`\`\`

## Location Words (場所の言葉)

Chapter 5 introduces essential position words:

| Japanese | Reading | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| 上 | うえ (ue) | on, above |
| 下 | した (shita) | under, below |
| 中 | なか (naka) | inside |
| 外 | そと (soto) | outside |
| 前 | まえ (mae) | in front of |
| 後ろ | うしろ (ushiro) | behind |
| 隣 | となり (tonari) | next to |
| 近く | ちかく (chikaku) | near |
| 間 | あいだ (aida) | between |

### Using の to Connect

Pattern: **[Object A] の [Position] に [Object B] が あります/います**

\`\`\`
机の上に本があります。
(Tsukue no ue ni hon ga arimasu.)
There is a book on the desk.

椅子の下に猫がいます。
(Isu no shita ni neko ga imasu.)
There is a cat under the chair.

会社の近くにコンビニがあります。
(Kaisha no chikaku ni konbini ga arimasu.)
There is a convenience store near the office.
\`\`\`

## Mistakes I Made

### 1. Mixing います and あります

At first, I'd use あります for people:
- ❌ 田中さんがあります (Wrong — people use います)
- ✅ 田中さんがいます (Correct)

**Rule of thumb:** If it can move by itself → います. If not → あります.

### 2. Forgetting に for Movement Verbs

- ❌ 会社行きます (Missing particle)
- ✅ 会社**に**行きます (Destination particle needed)

### 3. Using は Instead of が for Existence

- ❌ 本は机の上にあります (Feels unnatural)
- ✅ 本**が**机の上にあります (Correct — が introduces new information)

Claude Code caught all of these and explained *why* they were wrong, not just that they were wrong.

## Wife's Input

My wife (who passed N5) gave me this advice:

> "います and あります are confusing at first, but just remember: people and animals use います. Everything else uses あります. Even plants use あります because they don't move!"

She also taught me casual forms (いる/ある instead of います/あります), but told me to stick with formal forms for now since I'm learning for work contexts.
